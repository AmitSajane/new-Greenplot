Your 6 lease types — defined (this fixes the confusion)
💵Cash Rent
Farmer pays a fixed cash amount upfront for the season. Owner takes zero risk.
Owner entersTotal ₹ + pay-by date
Money flowLump-sum cash before sowing

📅Fixed Rent
Fixed ₹ per acre / year, paid in installments (e.g. 50% sowing, 50% harvest).
Owner enters₹/acre/yr + installments
Money flowScheduled fixed payments
Owner risk
🌾Crop Share
Owner & farmer split the physical harvest by a ratio (50:50, 60:40…). No fixed cash.
Owner entersShare % + input-cost split
Money flowProduce divided at harvest
Owner risk
💰Revenue Share
Owner gets a % of the sale revenue (cash from selling the crop), e.g. 25%.
Owner entersRevenue % + cost split
Money flow% of money after the sale
Owner risk
⚖️Flexible Share
Base rent + bonus that flexes with yield/price. Base is safe; both share the upside.
Owner entersBase ₹/acre + bonus rule

Owner risk

Money flowWhatever is agreed

The loop, closed

The core fix: a Land has one or more Lease Offers. The owner sets terms per offer with a form that changes by lease type (Crop Share shows a split slider; Fixed Rent shows ₹/acre + installments; Custom shows a clause editor). The farmer sees those real offers, picks one, applies — then approve → agreement → e-sign → active.

Reuses your existing screens: AddFarmScreen, LeaseTypeDetails, LeaseDetailView, CompareLeases, LeaseApplication, LeaseConfirmation, LeaseStatus, MyActiveLeases, AgreementDetails, Tenants. New pieces: AddLeaseOffer (dynamic form), LeaseRequests (owner inbox), Agreement generator. The hardcoded mock lease lists get replaced by one shared LEASE_TYPES definition used by both sides.

Backend (Supabase): tables lands → lease_offers (type + terms jsonb) → lease_requests → leases (active) — exactly the chain in schema.sql.
